The Diagnosis

What Turnover Actually Costs, and Why Perks Don't Touch It

Replacing an experienced clinical hire typically costs somewhere north of a full year of her salary once recruiting, training, and lost productivity during ramp-up are counted, and that's before the disruption to patient continuity. For HR directors and CMOs, the pattern that leads there is familiar: a wellness stipend arrives, attendance at the optional session is thin, and the exit interviews six months later cite the same reasons they always did.

The research on why perks alone don't work is fairly consistent: employees who rate their culture poorly are roughly four times as likely to be job hunting as those who rate it well, a gap that shows up in eNPS and turnover long before it shows up in a resignation letter. Generic wellness perks target symptoms, not structural causes: unclear decision-making authority, no reliable channel to flag a problem before it compounds, and workload design that assumes nothing will go wrong on a given shift.

The Reframe

Training Built on Psychological Safety, Not Morale Events

Our training model treats psychological safety as infrastructure, not sentiment. That means building specific structures that let a front-desk coordinator flag a scheduling flaw, or a nurse raise a workflow concern, without it depending on which manager happens to be on shift that day. Paired with that is role-specific upskilling for clinical and administrative staff, built from the same diagnostic findings that inform our consultancy work, so training addresses the skill gaps this organization has. This is our Human & Behavioral Diagnostics method in its most direct form: psychological safety and role-specific skill aren't an afterthought here. They're the subject.

Equipment Handling & Maintenance Training

The Training That Cuts Your Equipment Service Bills

Most healthcare equipment failures aren't random. They're the accumulated result of daily handling: a device powered down incorrectly, equipment used outside its recommended operating parameters, small issues left unreported until they become expensive ones. None of that shows up as a training gap until the service bill arrives, and healthcare equipment service, both parts and labor, is expensive by design.

This training is built directly on our biomedical engineering consulting work, and it's specific to the equipment your facility uses rather than a generic handle-with-care session. Staff learn the manufacturer-specified operating parameters for their specific machines, the early warning signs that predict a failure before it happens, and the reporting habits that turn a small, fixable issue into a five-minute fix instead of a week of downtime. Multiple industry maintenance benchmarks put the gap between reactive repair and properly maintained equipment at roughly 40 to 60 percent in avoided repair and service cost, with broader hospital-facility research putting typical annual maintenance spend at 5 to 10 percent of total equipment value. Even the conservative end of that range is a meaningful line item on a hospital's equipment budget.

Executive & Leadership Coaching

Executive Coaching for the Person No One Else Is Coaching

Managers, CEOs, and CXOs are usually the last people in an organization to get structured support, even though the pressure of the role (decision fatigue, isolation, being the person everyone else brings problems to) is real. This is one-on-one and small-group coaching for senior leadership: motivation and stress management, decision-making under pressure, and staying current on the technology and strategic shifts relevant to running a healthcare organization.

This isn't a niche idea. The global executive coaching and leadership development market is valued at over 100 billion dollars and is one of the faster-growing categories of corporate spending, and roughly a third of Fortune 500 companies now use executive coaching as a standard part of developing senior leaders. The logic is straightforward: an organization invests heavily in training the people who report to a CEO or clinical director, then gives that same leader no equivalent support, despite the fact that their decision-making affects everyone below them. This closes that gap.

Financial Wellness & Literacy

Financial Stress Isn't a Personal Problem. It's a Productivity Problem.

Physical health, financial health, and mental health don't sit in separate boxes inside a workforce. They move together, and when one gets disrupted, the other two usually follow, along with overall productivity. PwC's Employee Financial Wellness Survey found 59 percent of employees are currently stressed about money, and financially stressed employees are five times more likely to be distracted at work. The same survey found that group also reports significantly worse mental health, worse sleep, and worse physical health than employees without that stress. Treating financial wellness as separate from the rest of employee wellness misses a connection the data is pretty clear about.

That's why financial wellness sits inside this training vertical instead of being left out or handled as a one-off perk. It runs in three tiers, matched to who each one is for.
