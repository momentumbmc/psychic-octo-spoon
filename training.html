The Diagnosis

What Turnover Actually Costs, and Why Perks Don't Touch It

Replacing an experienced clinical hire typically costs somewhere north of a full year of her salary once recruiting, training, and lost productivity during ramp-up are counted, and that's before the disruption to patient continuity. For HR directors and CMOs, the pattern that leads there is familiar: a wellness stipend arrives, attendance at the optional session is thin, and the exit interviews six months later cite the same reasons they always did.

The research on why perks alone don't work is fairly consistent: employees who rate their culture poorly are roughly four times as likely to be job hunting as those who rate it well, a gap that shows up in eNPS and turnover long before it shows up in a resignation letter. Generic wellness perks target symptoms, not structural causes: unclear decision-making authority, no reliable channel to flag a problem before it compounds, and workload design that assumes nothing will go wrong on a given shift.

The Reframe

Training Built on Psychological Safety, Not Morale Events

Our training model treats psychological safety as infrastructure, not sentiment. That means building specific structures that let a front-desk coordinator flag a scheduling flaw, or a nurse raise a workflow concern, without it depending on which manager happens to be on shift that day. Paired with that is role-specific upskilling for clinical and administrative staff, built from the same diagnostic findings that inform our consultancy work, so training addresses the skill gaps this organization has. This is our Human & Behavioral Diagnostics method in its most direct form: psychological safety and role-specific skill aren't an afterthought here. They're the subject.

The Training Model

Baseline Culture & Skills Assessment

A structured read on where psychological safety and role-specific competency currently stand, before any training is designed.

Role-Specific Upskilling

Curriculum built separately for clinical and administrative roles, based on the actual skill gaps identified in the assessment.

Psychological Safety Systems

Structural channels for raising concerns early, with clear ownership. Not a single workshop: an ongoing mechanism.

Manager Training on Burnout Prevention

Direct training for the managers whose day-to-day decisions most shape whether staff feel safe flagging a problem.

Replacing an experienced hire costs roughly 1.3 times her annual salary once every cost is counted. That's the number this vertical is built to move, and eNPS and culture ratings tend to shift months before the turnover number does, which is why we track those first.

The System

Where This Connects

Training only holds if the operational structure underneath it isn't working against it, which is why this vertical draws directly on the diagnostic findings from Healthcare Engineering rather than starting from a blank template. It's also easier to invest properly in training when procurement isn't quietly eating the margin that would otherwise fund it. Organizations overpaying for supplies often short-staff to compensate, one of the more common hidden drivers of the burnout this vertical exists to address.
