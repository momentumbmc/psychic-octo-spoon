The Diagnosis

The Spend No One's Reviewing

Hospital supply expenses per patient rose 18.5 percent between 2019 and 2022, outpacing inflation by nearly 30 percent, and surgical supplies alone typically run about 15 percent of a hospital's total operating costs. Most of that spend gets set once, in a contract or a purchase order, and then reordered on autopilot for years. Nobody revisits whether the packing wrap used in CSSD is still the right choice, whether a marginally different specification on a routine consumable would do the same clinical job for less, or whether something currently bought pre-made has a viable in-house alternative.

A peer-reviewed study of a tertiary care hospital's CSSD found that in-house preparation of just two consumable items saved roughly ₹94,000 a month between them.* Small, unglamorous line items. Large, compounding numbers.

The Reframe

Why Generic Cost-Cutters Miss This

The financial impact of clinical optimization is highly measurable. Recent studies utilizing machine learning approaches to analyze hospital networks demonstrated that optimizing diagnostic tests and managing resources can yield up to a 15-25% reduction in total operating costs. Data-driven cost optimization strategies frequently uncover unwarranted clinical variations. For example, audits routinely find high-cost surgical supplies that are opened in the operating room but never actually used. By pairing clinical judgment with precise procurement discipline, value analysis committees can stop this specific type of supply waste.

Our engineers review the technical data, our analysts review the financial impact, and we negotiate directly through our procurement network. We handle the friction so your management team can focus entirely on expansion and care delivery

Consumables & Disposables Audit

The Audit That Finds What Your Invoices Don't Show

This is a direct, department-by-department review of recurring consumable and disposable spend, starting with the highest-volume areas: CSSD, operating theatres, and general wards. Every recurring line item gets checked against three questions: does the current specification need to be this expensive to do its clinical job, is there an equivalent alternative that costs less, and is this something that could be made in-house instead of purchased pre-made. None of those questions get asked in a typical procurement cycle, where the default is to reorder what was ordered last time.

The kind of result this finds is well documented, not speculative. A peer-reviewed CSSD cost study found six-figure rupee monthly savings from just two reprocessed items. A separate multi-year hospital study found a modified inventory system cut weekly consumable costs by close to half. These aren't projections. They're what a proper audit of this specific spend category tends to turn up.

The Pouch-Versus-Wrap Decision Nobody Revisits

Lightweight instruments like forceps and scissors don''t always need a fully wrapped tray. Where a peel-open pouch does the same sterilization job, the material cost difference compounds across thousands of cycles a year, and it''s exactly the kind of swap that stays unexamined once a packing protocol is set and nobody''s asked to revisit it.

Surgical Supply Waste

The Supplies Opened and Never Used

Several independent studies, including a 219-surgeon multicenter survey and a departmental audit at UCSF, put unused-but-opened surgical supply waste at roughly a quarter of what gets opened for a given case. A preference-card review, matching what gets used to what gets opened, is a standard fix. One documented case cut waste on a single procedure type by over 90 percent.
